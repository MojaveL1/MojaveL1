02emission

Where testnet coins come from.

No premine on the testnet chain. Blocks pay their miner, and the curve is written into the node.

Every block pays the miner who found it, plus the fees of the transactions inside it. The reward falls smoothly rather than in halvings:

reward = (100,000,000 − emitted) / 2^19
then     2.0 tMOJAVE per block, forever

The tail matters. A chain that pays nothing eventually stops being mined, and a privacy chain with too few miners has a weak anonymity set. Two coins per block keeps the work going indefinitely.

Coinbase outputs mature after three blocks, and so does every output used in a ring, which is why a fresh faucet drip shows as locked for about ninety seconds.

03getting some

Three ways to hold a testnet coin.

01

Faucet

Open the wallet, press the faucet button, and twenty five tMOJAVE arrives in a private transaction. One drip per address and per network, once an hour, so the node is not drained by a script.

02

Mine in the browser

Open /mine, pick your threads and leave the tab running. Rewards go straight to the address you paste in, which can be a quantum address.

03

Mine from a terminal

The CLI miner talks to any node, yours or the public one. It fetches a block template, solves Argon2id on every core and submits the nonce.

04disclosure

What this page does not say.

read this before you buy anything

tMOJAVE has no value and cannot be sold. Nothing here is an offer to sell tokens or a promise of a return. This page does not list prices, venues or allocations, because those are not published here.

The chain is a testnet run by one node. The code has not been independently audited. The privacy claims on this site are testable in your browser, and you should test them rather than trust them.

If you want the technical detail, the whitepaper is a ten minute read and the repository is small enough to skim in an evening.
